How fast the voice talks (1.0× = normal, 1.5× = 50% faster). Changes tempo only, not pitch.
How high or low the voice is, in semitones (12 = one octave). Applied after the voice is generated; speed and voice size stay the same. Negative = lower.
Apparent size of the speaker's throat and mouth, independent of pitch. Negative = bigger, deeper, more adult/male. Positive = smaller, younger, cuter.
Softens the hissy, airy high frequencies (above ~3 kHz). Higher = less breath and hiss, but consonants like s/sh get duller. Try 0.3–0.6.
Random variation in the voice's tone and pitch contour. Low = flatter and steadier, a bit robotic. High = livelier but can sound wobbly or unstable. piper-plus docs: "audio variation control"; they recommend 0.5 for Tsukuyomi (their CLI default is 0.4).
Random variation in timing: how long each sound and pause lasts. Low = metronome-steady. High = more human-sounding but uneven. piper-plus docs: "phoneme duration variation"; their CLI default is 0.5.
